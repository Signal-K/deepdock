I've come up with a new idea for a project - inspired by `SuperLocal`. Basically, an app that can track your "adventures" or just daily life and recommend new places to visit, through a combination of reinforcement learning & generative AI.
Some ideas would be each city or country could be represented as a fictional location, created via a colour overlay that is rendered on top of the map. The big question would be the cost of creating such an app, in terms of integrating with an API for the maps capacity. I could probably access the OpenAI API for chat completion for a few dollars here and there.

The first job: define the problem and the solution.
The problem is that we don't really have a good way to "adventurise" your travels or daily life.
When people go on holiday (unless they're going to remote locations), it can get overwhelming deciding where to go, what to do, etc to make the most of your day.
When you're at home (for most people), you're going to have days that you need to fill with something as you've got a gap in your schedule. Or you're wanting to make your commute to work or school more interesting. 
This is an app that will make your day more interesting, your holiday more fulfilling and your life richer.
(Had to figure out a 3 phase slogan....fml)

It will consist of a few main parts:
1. It will track where you've been and where you're going
	1. We will require location services permissions from users for this


Potentially, it could help with driving users towards citizen science hotspots? Check-ins?
Create simple minigames at different landmarks?
I want to look into games like Tiny Town & Teeny Tiny Trains for possible public infrastructure projects...

Here's the thing. I originally had a four-point goal for Star Sailors V2 that we kind of achieved...in that we got a bit of everything done.
There are some features I would love to introduce, e.g. #AreWeAlone and more building/construction, but I think I'll pause work on new features and commit entirely to whatever is going to bring in an audience that's going to stick around. However, the new features & patches I would have been proposing in the [[Segment-V2.1-Bug-Fixes]] segment may still be relevant, as it's not entirely clear what would be the best way to attract new users.
1. 100% native mobile app
	1. Simply because mobile would be the way most people would be interacting with #Star-Sailors, as we're suggesting a "bite-sized" approach would be the simplest way for "newbies" to get involved in citizen science. Until there's enough game content & mechanics, it would be hard to get people to be playing Star Sailors on the computer for long stretches
	2. With the continued focus on mobile (which I already sort of started during the final few months of work on #V2), it's going to continue to improve the experience and make things simpler
2. General-purpose UI improvements
	1. Things like images fitting into their containers perfectly
	2. Parity across desktop & mobile
3. Notifications
	1. Brings users back into the game
4. New projects like Click-A-Coral, Are We Alone, Buzzing Bees, etc
	1. This would enable more world building and player interaction & creation
	2. E.g. users would be able to go fishing on worlds they discover, building settlements through a combination of fictional narrative points and classification data providing capacity to perform these points/actions
Overall, I think that the best thing to do is to focus only on minor UI improvements, get as much user feedback as possible and focus on features that relate to that feedback. 
In terms of the circular data pipeline, I'm okay with spending some time on the data scraping aspect, but until we get returning users, I'm not going to work on the second part of it. 
The visual side of the world building will be done through the new minigames that I hope to debut very early next year ^. 

Probably the only new feature that I can state categorically will need to be included will be the labelling of what is "real" and "narrative-driven". Everything else will be decided after consultation with users, potentially including paying for random users to sign up and play the whole way through across multiple device types.
